GERF Radar Pong: Contactless UWB Radar-Controlled Game

Real-time interactive game developed for public engagement at Imperial College London and the UK Dementia Research Institute Care Research & Technology Centre. A commercial UWB X4 radar sensor tracks the player's torso distance at 500 frames/second, mapping position directly to paddle movement — no controllers, cameras, or wearables required. Radar backscatter is processed using STFT-based Range–Doppler analysis with real-time peak detection.

Features progressive difficulty scaling: ball speed and count increase with hits, paddle height shrinks, and special bonus balls and combo multipliers are introduced. Designed to make radar sensing technology tangible and accessible to public audiences, while demonstrating its potential for contactless human–computer interaction and health monitoring applications.

May 2025 – Jan 2026

Unified Gait Analysis Across UWB and FMCW Radars

Applied and validated the radar-based gait analysis framework previously proposed in Hadjipanayi et al. (IEEE TBME, 2024) across two heterogeneous radar modalities — impulse-radio ultra-wideband (IR-UWB) and frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) — to assess its generalisability in extracting spatiotemporal parameters without technology-specific tuning. The framework was evaluated using collocated radar systems operating under identical conditions with ten healthy participants performing overground walking trials.

Introduced a technology-independent walking segment identification method enabling consistent gait event detection across radar modalities. Extracted parameters — stride time, stride length, walking speed, swing time, and stance time — were validated against gold-standard optical motion capture using Pearson correlation, Bland–Altman, and intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) analyses.

Both radar modalities achieved 85–98% accuracy with inter-modality differences below 4.1%, demonstrating equivalent performance across all parameters. Results support the feasibility of a unified processing framework deployable across diverse radar hardware, advancing towards modality-independent radar platforms for continuous gait monitoring.

Jan 2023 – Jan 2024

Radar-Based Asymmetric Gait Analysis Using UWB Sensors

Investigated the use of commercially available UWB radar sensors for detecting and quantifying gait asymmetries during overground locomotion. An adjustable knee brace was applied to simulate five levels of gait impairment in 10 able-bodied participants during a 6-metre walking task. Six XeThru X4M03 UWB radars captured backscattered signals for joint Range–Doppler–Time (RDT) analysis.

Developed a computationally efficient signal processing framework to extract seven spatiotemporal gait parameters from the multi-radar RDT representation. Gait asymmetry was quantified via the symmetry ratio of step times. Validated against gold-standard optical motion capture, the system achieved 88.2–98.8% accuracy across all parameters, and 95.6 ± 2.8% accuracy in capturing step time symmetry ratios — sufficient to distinguish between different gait impairment levels.

Demonstrated the feasibility of UWB radar for unobtrusive, contactless detection of gait asymmetries during natural overground walking, with direct relevance to in-home monitoring of frailty biomarkers and neurological or musculoskeletal conditions.

Jan 2021 – Nov 2023

Radar-Based Spatiotemporal Gait Analysis Using UWB Sensors

Developed a novel radar-based gait analysis framework for extracting clinically relevant spatiotemporal parameters from overground walking using commercially available UWB radar sensors. Introduced a joint Range–Doppler–Time (RDT) representation and signal processing pipeline for contactless gait event detection and parameter quantification.

Algorithms designed and implemented include: radar signal pre-processing, clutter suppression, Doppler analysis, gait event detection, multilateration, and gait parameter extraction. Validated against gold-standard optical motion capture with healthy participants, achieving high accuracy in estimating step/stride time, cadence, step/stride length, walking speed, swing/stance time, gait variability, and inter-limb symmetry.

Demonstrated the feasibility of radar as a privacy-preserving, unobtrusive alternative to laboratory-based gait assessment, supporting future deployment in continuous in-home mobility monitoring.

Jul – Dec 2022

Radar Simulation via Kinematic Motion-Matching Synthesis

Developed a radar signal simulation framework using the motion matching algorithm, widely used in the video game industry for character animation, as an alternative to analytical walking models such as the Boulic-Thalmann model. The method enables synthesis of kinematic data for non-periodic and abnormal movements such as limping, unstable walking, and aided gait, which cannot be replicated with analytical approaches.

Kinematic data were generated from the LAFAN1 motion capture dataset in collaboration with Dr. Balint Hodossy, then transformed into radar signals for the XeThru X4 sensor in MATLAB. Each of 19 body parts was modelled as a point target with ellipsoidal radar cross-section (RCS), producing Range-Doppler-Time (RDT) representations and Doppler spectrograms consistent with those from analytical models.

The framework offers key advantages including support for multi-directional walking, a greater number of body parts, and the ability to synthesise diverse motion patterns such as gait abnormalities and fall-inducing movements. Future extensions include combining motion matching with physics engines and reinforcement learning to simulate human–environment interactions, with applications in fall detection and gait disorder analysis for neurological conditions.

Jan 2020 – Mar 2020

Adaptive Signal Processing and Machine Intelligence

Developed and evaluated advanced adaptive signal processing and machine intelligence algorithms in MATLAB, covering spectrum estimation, adaptive filtering, system identification, time-frequency analysis, and dimensionality reduction techniques. Implemented and analysed LMS, Leaky LMS, NLMS, GASS, GNGD, CLMS, and ACLMS adaptive algorithms for real-time signal modelling, noise cancellation, frequency estimation, and non-stationary signal tracking. Applied principal component analysis (PCA) and principal component regression (PCR) for robust regression and denoising, and investigated modern spectral estimation techniques including MUSIC, autoregressive modelling, and adaptive spectrum estimation. Demonstrated methods on biomedical, EEG, ECG, respiratory, wind-speed, and power-system datasets.

This work was done as part of the Adaptive Signal Processing and Machine Intelligence module of the MEng degree in Bioengineering at Imperial College London.

Feb 2020 – Mar 2020

Neural Decoding for Hand Trajectory Prediction

Developed a causal neural decoder in MATLAB to estimate hand movement trajectories from neural spike train recordings. Implemented and evaluated multiple classifiers, including k-Nearest Neighbours (k-NN), Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Bayesian approaches, achieving 99.75% reaching-angle classification accuracy through majority voting. Principal Component Regression (PCR) was used for hand trajectory estimation, outperforming Non-Linear Least Mean Squares (N-LMS) in both accuracy and computational efficiency.

This work was done as part of a group project for the Brain Machine Interfaces module during the MEng degree at Imperial College London.

Contactless Vital Sign Monitoring Using Doppler Radar

Developed MATLAB algorithms for contactless estimation of heart rate and respiratory rate using Doppler radar signals. The project involved signal acquisition, preprocessing, digital filtering, spectral analysis, and physiological parameter extraction from radar measurements. Implemented and evaluated multiple frequency estimation techniques, including FFT, Welch, Burg, Yule-Walker, and Lomb-Scargle spectral estimation methods. Simultaneously processed ECG recordings to derive reference heart rate and respiration measurements for algorithm validation and performance assessment.

This work was done as part of a research internship at the University of Cyprus, under the supervision of Prof. Constantinos Pitris.

Oct 2018 – Jun 2019

Bioreactor Instrumentation for Mechanical Cell Stimulation

Designed instrumentation for a perfusion bioreactor used to study the effects of mechanical strain on cultured cells, and to investigate how mechanical strain influences adipogenesis and adipocyte mechanobiology in vitro. Developed a programmable linear actuation system capable of applying cyclic strains between 0.1–10 Hz, alongside a dual-syringe perfusion system for continuous nutrient delivery. The system was designed to stimulate multiple cell culture samples simultaneously, enabling different mechanical strain conditions to be applied across individual wells within a multi-well culture plate.

This work was done as part of the Third Year Group Project for the MEng degree in Bioengineering at Imperial College London.
